Status of This Memo

This memo announces a 90-minute, in-person meet-up accepted to CHI 2027, the ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, held in Pittsburgh, PA, USA, on 10–14 May 2027. Distribution of this memo is unlimited: the meet-up is open to all CHI attendees and needs no submission, preparation, programming, or device.

The date, time, and room will be posted here once the CHI 2027 program is published.

1.

Why Protocol Designers, Why Now

Much of HCI is organized around platforms: one operator hosts the service, holds the data, and writes and enforces the rules. A protocol instead specifies how parties built and run by different people interact without a single operator, as in email and the web. Designing a protocol means designing collective behavior at a distance: deciding who may do what, what counts as a valid action, what is recorded, and how the rules can change.

Protocol design is not new to our community. CSCW has long studied coordination mechanisms, and CHI researchers practice protocol design today in federated social media, community governance tools, personal data stores, and cooperative platforms. As AI agents begin to search, negotiate, purchase, and delegate on people's behalf at machine speed, a growing number of researchers also turn to protocols to govern and coordinate AI.

We call all of them protocol designers, whether they write specifications, study protocols in use, or critique them. Their work is split across sessions on social computing, AI, and governance. This meet-up gives them a place at CHI to find one another and to start a shared conversation on how protocols should handle authority, change, and failure as AI agents join them.

2.

Guiding Questions

  • Authority

    Who sets the rules, and who can change them?

  • Change

    How are protocols amended or forked?

  • Failure

    What happens when rules break, and which rule breaks first when agents join?

All prompts are available in large print on the materials page.

3.

Who Should Come

Anyone who designs or studies protocols or infrastructures, or imagines their futures through speculative design, worlding, or world-building. At the meet-up, each attendee takes a badge colored by their main community:

  • Social mediaDecentralized and federated social media, feed curation, moderation, server administration.
  • GovernanceCommunity governance tools, AI governance, institutional design.
  • AgentsAI agents, agent infrastructure, multi-agent interaction.
  • Spatial computingSpatial computing and its standardization.
  • Economies and Web3Platform cooperatives, alternative economies, Web3.
  • Standards and infrastructureStandards, infrastructure studies, open-source software.

We also welcome speculative and critical designers, scholars of law and economics who treat rules as designed artifacts, and students looking for an entry point. No technical background is required: you need not program, hold an AI account, or bring a device.

5.

Accessibility

Large-print and screen-readable versions of the prompts and the wall map are on the materials page. All activities can be done seated: organizers place notes on the wall map for anyone who prefers not to stand, and you may stay at one table throughout. You can contribute in writing as well as by speaking. No photographs or recordings are taken without consent. Latecomers can introduce themselves at the wall map and join any round.

6.

After the Meet-Up

With attendees' consent, organizers photograph the wall map and table notes. After the conference, we publish a short summary, the shared reading list, and contact channels on this site, so that the map of the field outlives the session. Attendees can join a mailing list at the closing, and volunteers will form a group to propose a CHI 2028 workshop.

Questions before then: Botao Amber Hu, University of Oxford.

Large-print materials

These are the prompts and the wall map used at the CHI 2027 meet-up Meeting Protocol Designers at CHI: Towards Distributed Autonomous Futures, in a form you can enlarge, print, or read with a screen reader. You can answer any prompt in writing or by speaking.

Three guiding questions

  1. Authority. Who sets the rules in your protocol, and who can change them?
  2. Change. How is your protocol amended or forked?
  3. Failure. What happens when the rules of your protocol break?

Introduce yourself

In about one minute, say who you are and finish this sentence:

  1. “The protocol I design or study is …”

Provocation cards

During mapping, organizers pose one provocation per guiding question. Write your answer on a sticky note or share it aloud; organizers can place your note on the map for you.

  1. Who can change the rules in your protocol, and how?
  2. What does your protocol do when a participant misbehaves?
  3. Which rule breaks first when agents join?
  4. Which decisions should an agent never make for a person?
  5. What did you learn from a protocol that failed?

Badge colors

Choose the badge for your main community. Each color has a matching region on the wall map.

  • Red: Social media
  • Blue: Governance
  • Ochre: Agents
  • Green: Spatial computing
  • Magenta: Economies and Web3
  • Teal: Standards and infrastructure

The wall map, described

The wall map is a large sheet of paper divided into six regions, one per badge color. During self-introductions, each attendee places one pin in the region of their community, labelled with the protocol they design or study.

During mapping, attendees add sticky notes with their answers to the provocations, then link their pin with a thread to pins from other communities that face the same problem. Clusters of linked pins become the tables for field building.

All activities can be done seated. Organizers place notes and pins on the map for anyone who prefers not to stand.
